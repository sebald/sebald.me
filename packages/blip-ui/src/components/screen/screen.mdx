# Screen

The frame of a full screen: a menu, a shop, a list of saves, a title card, two windows side by side.
It fills the stage, keeps clear of the bar of controls on the tall stage,
and places what is in it as a column with the stage's spacing, so a screen
has no `position`, `inset` or `--bui-bar` of its own.

```tsx
<Stage>
  <Screen>
    <ScreenTitle blurb="Spend it before the sprint ends.">Shop</ScreenTitle>
    <Window>…</Window>
  </Screen>
</Stage>
```

## Anatomy

1. **The frame**, the stage's whole layer, padded by 6 `--bui-px`
2. **The bottom**, which also clears the bar of controls (`--bui-bar`), 0 on
   the wide stage
3. **The title**, optional: a `ScreenTitle` as the first child is the
   column's first row, 8 stage pixels from the top as it always was, and
   the rows under it follow whatever its height (a blurb, the tall stage's
   larger letters)
4. **The column**, what else is in it, one under the other, 4 `--bui-px`
   apart; in the default variant a `Window` takes the full width

## Usage

- **Do** make a screen's top-level box a `Screen`, the first child of the
  `Stage`, and put the rest in it.
- **Do** leave out the padding, the gap and the top clearance for the title:
  the frame has them. A one-off placement is a `className` on the part, not
  on the frame.
- **Do** use `variant="centered"` for one group in the middle, a title card
  or a message.
- **Do** use `variant="split"` for two windows side by side, never a flex
  row or a grid of your own: upright they stack by themselves.
- **Don't** write `position: absolute`, `inset` or `--bui-bar` on a screen's
  box. The frame is the only box that knows the bar.
- **Don't** put a `Screen` inside a `Window` or another `Screen`.
- **Don't** put a `Dialog` in a `Screen`'s column to cover the stage; it
  covers the stage wherever it is, and the frame is not a place for it to
  sit.

## Variants

`default` is a column from the top down. `centered` puts one group in the
middle of the stage and its text in the middle of the group, with 8
`--bui-px` around it. It is the same frame, so a screen clears the bar either
way. `Stage.Center` stays as it is, for a group that is not a screen's frame;
a screen starts with `Screen`.

`split` holds two windows: side by side on the wide stage, each as tall as
the room under the title, `section` apart, and one above the other on the
tall stage, `regular` apart as the column's rows are. A shop's wares and the bag, two saves
to compare, a list and what its row shows. Each window is its own: its
title names it, its legend says its keys, and its list takes the keys when
the player last pointed at or tapped it (`Picker`, several on one layer).
Each list scrolls in its own room. Write the two windows in the order they
are read: the left one first, which upright is the upper one, so a screen
reader hears them as they are seen. It is for exactly two: one window is
the `default` column, and more than two is no `split`.

```tsx
<Screen variant="split">
  <ScreenTitle>Shop</ScreenTitle>
  <Window>…Buy…</Window>
  <Window>…Sell…</Window>
</Screen>
```

## A list longer than the stage

The frame is as tall as the stage, so a window in it is never taller.
Given more rows than fit, the window's list takes the room that is left
and scrolls: the screen's title, the window's title, what it says and its
legend stay. The arrows keep the cursor's row in view, jumping to it, as
list navigation doesn't move; a finger drags the list, and the rail
beside it shows where it is. The smallest wide stage a game shows is
2.25x (`MIN_SCALE`; below it the game asks for a bigger window), where
this shop shows eleven of its twelve rows, and nine upright on a phone.
The docs' 1x shot is smaller than any game's, so it has room for two and
a half.

## Input

None: the screen is a box. Keys belong to what is in it (`Picker`, `useKeys`).

## Accessibility

- It renders a plain `div` with no role; the stage is the region and its
  `ScreenTitle` is the heading
- Reading order is the column's order, which is why the title is first
