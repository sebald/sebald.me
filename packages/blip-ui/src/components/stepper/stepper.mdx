# Stepper

The form's one choice control: a setting with its value between two step
arrows, `Music ◀ On ▶`, `Text speed ◀ Normal ▶`. On and Off, Normal and
Hard, Slow and Fast are all a stepper, as on an era game's options screen,
so there is nothing to choose between: the toggle, the option and the
settings row of a form are this. `StepperControl` is the value and
its arrows, the control of a `Field`; `Stepper` is the commonest form of
it, a `Field` with a label holding one: its children are the label,
`description` and `error` the field's words, the rest goes to the control.

```tsx
<Stepper name="music" values={['Off', 'On']} defaultValue="On">
  Music
</Stepper>

// the same, composed
<Field name="music">
  <FieldLabel>Music</FieldLabel>
  <StepperControl values={['Off', 'On']} defaultValue="On" />
</Field>
```

## Anatomy

1. **Label**, its children, the `Field`'s label: in the window's label
   column on a wide stage; on a tall one, or outside a window's rows,
   beside the value too, the value at the row's end, as on a phone's
   settings screen. The cursor
   points at it while the stepper has the keys' focus
2. **Left step** and **right step**, the `Setting`'s pixel triangles (`Step`),
   shown only where Left and Right work: in `--bui-primary` while it has
   the keys' focus, a second cue beside the cursor, and under the pointer.
   At rest a row is its name and value, as on a game's
   options screen, so a window of steppers is not a column of arrows; their room
   is kept, so nothing shifts when they show. Under a finger there is no
   cursor to show them: they stay, dim, as the sign that a tap steps it
3. **Value**, bright, centred between the arrows, which take the
   control's whole column: the arrows line up with a text field's box,
   every stepper's value sits on one centre, and the whole cell is the
   target for a tap. Six characters at least
4. **Description** and **error**, the `Field`'s, under the row from the
   label's start across its width, as a phone's settings screen explains
   a setting: they are about the row, not about the value. A
   `TextField`'s stay under its box, with its count

## Usage

- **Do** use it for every choice in a form, two values or seven. A menu
  row that is a setting is a `PickerSetting`, which looks the same and
  takes the `Picker`'s keys.
- **Do** write a setting that is on or off as `['Off', 'On']`, in that
  order, never Yes and No or Enabled and Disabled: only those words
  make it a switch to a screen reader. Two other
  values (`['Normal', 'Hard']`) are read as their words, so name the row
  for the choice: `Difficulty`, not `Hard mode`.
- **Do** write more values in the order Right goes through them: Slow,
  Normal, Fast. It goes round: Right from the last is the first again.
- **Don't** use it for a number the player only reads (a `Readout`), or a
  choice among actions or places (a `Picker` in a menu window).

## Input

- Left and Right step it, back and on, and go round at the ends
- Enter, Space and a tap step it on; on two values that is a flip. A tap
  on its label does the same, so the whole row is the target
- Home and End go to the first and the last value
- Up and Down are not its own: in a `Form` they move to the row above or
  below
- A disabled stepper takes none of them and is not a stop
- Each step plays the `move` sound through `useFeedback()`; the value
  changes at once, as list navigation does: no motion

## Accessibility

- `['Off', 'On']`: `role="switch"`, `aria-checked` when on. Any other
  values, two included: a `role="slider"` across
  (`aria-orientation="horizontal"`), its value in words
  (`aria-valuetext`), so Normal and Hard are heard as Normal and Hard, not
  as off and on. The words on the stage are the same and are read once
- Named by its `Field`'s label (`aria-labelledby`), the name the same in
  every state; described by the field's description and error
- A change is said by the role's own state, which a screen reader reads
  for the control it is on, not by the stage's live region, which would
  say it twice
- A Tab stop; the focus is the `Field`'s cursor left of the label and the
  arrows showing beside the value, in primary (`foundations/focus.mdx`)
- The value is in words, so the state never rests on colour. The arrows
  are 10.51:1 on the window with the focus (primary) and 3.05:1 at rest
  under a finger (dim, the 3:1 of WCAG 1.4.11); the value is bright,
  11.56:1
- A touch screen gets a row at least 44 CSS pixels high
- `name` sends the value shown (`On`, `Fast`) with the form
