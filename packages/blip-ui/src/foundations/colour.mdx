# Colour

## Yellow says "look here"

The primary colour, yellow (`--bui-primary`), is the one the player
follows. Wherever it is, there is something to look at or do: **the
cursor, the focus, and the next step.** A player who loses track of a
screen finds their place again by finding the yellow, so it is kept for
that and spent on nothing else.

Level designers paint ledges and handholds yellow for the same reason
(the "yellow paint" of Uncharted, Tomb Raider and Horizon): it is the
colour the eye goes to first, and a scene rarely has it, so it stands out
without a word. CD Projekt chose it as Cyberpunk 2077's colour because it
grabs attention and is almost absent from its genre. The cost is the same
in a level as on a screen: the more yellow there is, the less any of it
says. A screen with three yellow things has no "here".

What is yellow:

- **The cursor**: a menu row's, a button's, a field's
- **The focus' second cue**: a text field's underline
  while it is typed in, a tab's underline and a panel's frame
- **The next step**: the menu item to pick first (`PickerItem` with
  `variant="accent"`), the call to press a key (`Text variant="call"`)
- **Whose line it is**: the player's speaker mark on a `Line`, art of
  the player's side as below

What is not:

- A button's face: the main action is a paper key, the quieter one its
  label alone, and the cursor beside one says which has the focus
  (`Button`)
- Frames, headings, titles, a window's ground, a large area
- A hint, whatever its state: a `Field`'s characters left stay muted when
  the field is full; the 0 says it, and yellow there would pull the eye
  from the field the player is typing in
- A chosen state: the open tab is raised and bright, not yellow; a
  pointer over something lightens it, not yellow
- Something only to make a screen livelier; the game's art has its own
  palette, and the player's own pieces in it (a meter of their side, a
  timer's face, a bitmap "WIN") are art, not a sign

## Do and don't

- **Do** keep one yellow sign per screen that asks for the player: the
  focus, or the next step when nothing has the focus yet
- **Do** point at what is next with the yellow you have (the cursor on
  it, the accent item) rather than a new mark
- **Don't** colour a control yellow to make it look important: place it
  first, give it the focus, and the yellow follows
- **Don't** rely on yellow alone: the cursor is a shape, the call is in
  words, so a player who sees no colour loses nothing (WCAG 1.4.1)
