# Focus

One mark says "you are here": **the menus' cursor, in the primary colour,
3 stage pixels left of what it points at.** A menu row, a button, an icon
button and a form field (left of its label) all show it, so a player
stepping through a screen follows one sign, as on a game's menus, where the
pointer stays at the row's name whatever the row holds. Nothing grows: the
cursor sits in room the layout leaves for it, and a focused control is
exactly as big as an unfocused one.

A control may add a second cue of its own, inside its box, that changes
colour without growing: a field's underline turns gold. The second cue is
for whoever misses a small pointer (the Xbox guidelines ask for more than
one cue). A few controls can't hold a cursor yet and keep
a line or a frame (Exceptions, below).

It shows for the keyboard only (`:focus-visible`), and in a game the keys
and the d-pad move a cursor of their own (`Picker`), which is the same sign.
Under a finger there is no cursor: a tap is its own pointer, and a field
shows its caret and its underline.

## The cursor's room

A cursor (a `Picker` row's, a `Button`'s, an `IconButton`'s, a `Field`'s) has to show
plainly what it points at, and it needs room to do that:

1. **One gap.** It sits 3 stage pixels from what it points at: a menu
   row's label, a field's label, a button's face (its dark ink edge counts as space, it
   barely shows on the window), an icon.
2. **Twice as far from the rest.** Nothing else it could point at is
   closer than twice that, 6, on its side: grouping by nearness asks for
   1.5 to 2 times the space between groups as within one, and this takes
   the top of it. At 1.3 times (buttons 8 apart) it read as the button
   before's.
3. **Clear of its frame.** The window or box it sits in is no closer than
   the 3: it shows, it doesn't touch the frame.

So focusable things side by side are 12 apart (`Actions`, the
`cursor-apart` role, `spacing.mdx`), and a row of them leaves room for the first one's cursor
(`Actions` starts 8 in, where a menu's labels start). `Content` starts its
form rows 8 in too (a `Field`, a `Readout`), so a window's
labels, menu items and buttons share one cursor column. The Blip visual tests
check every story at every scale for it, focused or not (`cursorRoom` in
`tests/visual/layout.ts`): against other cursors' targets, text, fields,
icons and the frame. Art and shapes drawn with borders or backgrounds are
not measured, so look at a new screen's cursors too.

## The mark

| Class            | Is                                                                                                  | For                             |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `bui-focus`      | An outline one `--bui-px` thick with a negative offset: a frame inside the edge                     | A box: a tab's panel            |
| `bui-focus-line` | An underline one `--bui-px` thick, two under the letters (text decoration, so it lengthens nothing) | A line of text: a `Tab`'s label |

The cursor is the mark; these two are for the exceptions. A `Button` and
an `IconButton` show the cursor, not a frame: one mark on every variant and over any scene, where a frame on the face would have to change with its colour
(gold on gold shows nothing). `Actions` leaves it room. A control whose own
face is the primary colour can still set `--bui-focus-color` to its
letters' colour and move the frame further in (`--bui-focus-inset`). A game
changes the mark by changing `--bui-primary`, not by a role of its own; it
has the same colour as the game's accent everywhere.

`foundations/uniformity.test.ts` fails on a focus style outside this table:
an outline outside the box, a ring, a fill that lifts (an icon button's ink fill darkens), a colour of its own.

## Decisions

Numbers are WCAG contrast ratios for the default palette, which Pushback's
palette equals (its colours are the same values), measured with the relative
luminance formula. Window is `--bui-surface`, `#131829`; ink is `--bui-ink`,
`#0b0d16`; primary is `#f2c14e`.

- **2.4.7 Focus Visible (AA):** every control below shows the mark.
- **1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (AA):** the mark is 3:1 or more against what
  is next to it, here 4.17:1 to 11.92:1 (the destructive key's frame is 2.68:1 against the light row on its outside, so one side carries it).
- **1.4.1 Use of Color, Xbox 103:** never colour alone. A line, a frame or a
  cursor _appears_ where there was none, or gets thicker.
- **2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA):** noted per control, not a gate. It asks
  for a mark whose area is at least a 2 CSS pixel perimeter of the control,
  with a 3:1 change between the focused and the unfocused pixels. The mark is
  1 `--bui-px` = 3 CSS pixels or more at every scale (`--bui-min-px`), so
  thickness always passes; area passes for a frame and fails for a line under
  a short label or a small cursor.

## Exceptions

Where a cursor doesn't fit yet, the control keeps a line or a frame
(decided 2026-11-24, to be redrawn later if a screen asks for it):

- **`Tab`**: a cursor needs 9 stage pixels left of a label and the next tab
  is closer; on a tall stage, where the tabs' art is finer, closer still. It
  keeps `bui-focus-line`
- **`TabPanel`**: what it marks is a whole window, nothing to point at. It
  keeps `bui-focus`
- **Pushback's role tiles and glossary tabs**: 8 apart where the cursor's
  room asks for 12, and the tile's frame already reads as the selection

There are no links in the kit. A link in a docs page is the browser's.

Pushback's own controls (a role's tile on the select screen, a glossary
tab) use the same `bui-focus` frame, inside the tile, in the gold of the
game, instead of the orange outline it had.

## Accessibility

- Every focusable part of the kit shows the mark under `:focus-visible`:
  from the keyboard and not after a tap or a click
- Shown by a line, frame or cursor that appears or thickens, and by its
  colour besides: never by colour alone
- No part is bigger focused than not, so zoom and layout are not disturbed
- Under forced colours the mark is an outline, which the browser keeps; the
  underline and the cursor follow the text colour
