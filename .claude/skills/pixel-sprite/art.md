# Pushback: art rules

From the game's spec (removed with `docs/plans`): the rules every scene and sprite follows. The prompts that apply them are in `prompts.md`.

## Backgrounds

Four scenes, one per opponent role and one for the boss, all made with the `pixel-header` pipeline: Gemini prompt with a header as style reference, `snap.mjs` to the native grid, a per-scene loop script for two or three living details. Target 480 by 270 native pixels (16:9, no 5:2 crop this time; Gemini draws the 2752 px render as 480 columns of 5.735 px, `snap.mjs` fits that pitch and gets 478 by 266, and `scene.mjs` pads it to 480 by 270 by repeating the plain edge columns and rows, which is why the edges must stay plain; the first cut at a whole 6 px grid came out 458 wide, squashed and drifting, and the loop scripts' coordinates were remapped when it was redone), the same 32-colour, 3 to 4 shades per material look as the headers. Scenes carry no people; the sprites are the people.

| Scene           | Used for           | Time and palette                                     | Where the duellists stand                                              | Living details (looped)                                                             |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open-plan floor | Dev opponents      | Late afternoon, blue-grey with warm monitor light    | Between two desk islands, monitors behind them as plain dark areas     | A monitor flicker, a CI light blinking green then red, a plant leaf in the AC draft |
| Meeting room    | PM opponents       | Mid-morning, beige and whiteboard white              | In front of the whiteboard, table in the foreground framing the bottom | Projector fan dust in the beam, a coffee mug steaming, the whiteboard marker light  |
| Studio corner   | Designer opponents | Evening, warm wood and one cool desk lamp            | Beside a moodboard wall, printer on the left as a dark frame           | Lamp flicker, a sticky note lifting in a draft, a tablet screen pulsing on standby  |
| Corner office   | The Stakeholder    | Night, city lights behind glass, dark navy and amber | Desk between the duellists, window behind                              | City windows twinkling, phone screen glowing on the desk, rain streaks on the glass |

Composition rule for all four: the middle band from y=60 to y=225 stays uncluttered where the sprites stand (x=60 to 170 for the left duellist, x=310 to 420 for the right by default; the stand positions are per scene in data, `STANDS`, so a scene whose furniture creeps in can move its duellists by a few pixels instead of being rerolled: the studio's printer and desk, the office's cabinet). The top 40 px is a plain ceiling or sky and holds the two bars per duellist; the bottom 45 px a plain floor; the dialogue box overlays the floor. Dark framing shapes on the left and right edges, as in the headers. In the game the duellists stand 160 stage pixels apart (`STANDS`), the room centred between them; in the corner office the Stakeholder stands eight pixels closer, in front of the chair, with the room left where it was (`view`).

Title and selection screens reuse the corner office at a darker exposure (no new scene).

Prompt: the `pixel-header` template as is, with two changes. The composition paragraph becomes `16:9 frame, about 480 pixels wide, with an empty plain middle band where two figures will stand later`, and `No people` stays. Living details are staged exactly as the skill says: in front of plain dark areas so the loop script can draw them cleanly.

Output per scene: `native.png`, `scene.webp` (lossless, 8 fps, 12 s loop) and `poster.png` for reduced motion, in `packages/pushback/src/assets/scenes/<slug>/`. Unlike the headers, the Gemini original is kept, in `packages/pushback/src/assets/sources/`, as the reference for later changes to the scene.

## Art and animation

Nothing is drawn by hand. Gemini generates every image (free tier), `snap.mjs` pulls it onto the pixel grid, scripts in the repo do the compositing, palette work and animation, and Claude Code reviews and repairs the pixels. Sprites stay at the pixel size Gemini draws them. Gemini's 2752 by 1536 renders are 240 columns of 11.465 px, so `snap.mjs` fits a fractional pitch and phase (a whole-pixel grid drifted a cell every two cells and smeared every face right of the first) and the figures come out 76 to 80 px tall (the first Dev sheet, a 1376 px render, came out at 76 and was redrawn); sheets are cut from the Gemini originals in the ignored `sources/originals/`, not the recompressed copies; the height range for sheets is 70 to 85 px on a 72 by 112 cell, and roles must not differ in height for no reason (a sheet outside the range is rerolled, not scaled); two exceptions by decision, Bo and Kim, whose first roll came at a finer pitch (94 and 95 px) and was kept because a broad man and a tall woman a head above the rest read as builds and give the cast body types, so the test's ceiling is 96. Downscaling to 48 px lost the face and the headphones, so the stage is 480 by 270; a figure is then a bit under a third of the screen height, the classic adventure-game proportion. Same palette discipline as the headers (3 to 4 shades per material, dark outline on the silhouette only), same pixel size as the scene.

Why Gemini and not a sprite tool: the AI sprite services (PixelLab and similar) are paid, and the Claude Code pixel plugins only script Aseprite, which is paid or a self-compile; neither draws a better figure than a reference-steered Gemini prompt. Gemini holds a character steady inside one image, so every character is generated as a single model sheet, not frame by frame.

The prompts for every image are in `prompts.md`: fourteen generations before rerolls (seven model sheets, the ghost, four scenes, one prop sheet, one head sheet), each with the references to attach, the file name under `sources/` and what to reroll on.

Pipeline per character:

1. Prompt Gemini for one model sheet: the figure six times in a row (idle, talk-open, taunt, flinch, win, lose), same character, same scale, front three-quarter view, plain dark background, a header attached as the style reference, the pixel-header template with `No people` removed and `Six copies of the same figure side by side, identical in size and colours, each in a different pose` added. Reroll until the six are the same person. PM, Designer and Stakeholder use the same prompt with the Dev sheet attached as a second reference, so scale and pixel size match.
2. `snap.mjs` at the sheet's detected grid (6 px on the Dev render), nothing forced; all six poses share one grid because they come from one image.
3. `sheet.mjs` keys out the flat background (a flood from the border, plus the soft shadow under the feet), finds the figures as the six largest connected groups (so a hand reaching into the next figure's columns still belongs to its own), stands each one on the bottom row of a 72 by 112 cell, locks the palette to 32 colours by merging the closest shades (the sharp that ships with Next has no quantiser) and writes the sheet, its cell boxes as JSON and a 4x contact print. Rectangles in `patches.json` are painted over a cell after the cut, for a brand mark not worth a reroll (the energy drink's claw became two bands).
4. Claude Code reviews the contact print (it can view the PNG) against the checklist in the `pixel-sprite` skill: same height across poses, no stray single pixels, mouth and prop readable, palette within the scene. It fixes small faults in code (`sheet.mjs` has `setPx`-style helpers as in `loop.mjs`) and sends larger faults back to step 1 with a follow-up prompt in the same Gemini chat (`Keep this exactly, but …`).

Opponents are not new drawings: two model sheets per role (the two looks the player can pick) plus one for the Stakeholder, so seven Gemini sheets in total, and each colleague is drawn from one of its role's two sheets as it is. The browser builds each sprite once from its sheet (`packages/pushback/src/ui/sheets.ts`). Sprites are never recoloured in code: hue-based tints caught skin and hair next to the garment and left pixels see-through, so they were taken out; and nothing is pasted on: the hand-held items from a Gemini item sheet looked wrong in the hand, so they were dropped with the item sheet. A colleague that needs a look of its own gets a sheet of its own; until then the run draw keeps the twins apart (see The run). The head sheet (hood up, beard) was not generated. The onboarding buddy's ghost is an eighth sheet: three cells (hover, hover, point), no legs, keyed like the others and drawn at 0.7 opacity. Gemini drew it a quarter taller than the people however it was asked, so it is the one sheet that is scaled (nearest neighbour to 0.78): a pale, soft figure with no face detail to lose. The Gemini originals are kept in the repo as `packages/pushback/src/assets/sources/<id>.jpg`; files under `src/assets` only ship when imported, so nothing excludes them from the build, they are simply never imported. They are the reference image for every later prompt, and a snapped PNG works worse as one.

Animation is code, not frames:

| Pose     | Source                    | How it moves                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Idle     | Model sheet cell 1        | Breathing: two frames of about half a second, the feet planted, the body above the hip one pixel down on the exhale (`breathFrame`, cut at `BREATH.hip` of the figure's height from the sheet's cell box)                                                                                                                 |
| Talk     | Cell 2, mouth from cell 1 | The talk cell held (the hand stays up) while the line types, with the idle cell's lower face pasted over it in an uneven rhythm (`TALK`, about three openings a second); shut once the line is typed. `features.mjs` finds the face patch per sheet. Swapping whole cells every frame waved the hand eight times a second |
| Taunt    | Cell 3                    | 2 px step toward the opponent over 2 frames, then held with the shoulders shaking: the breathing cut at 400 ms (`BREATH.chuckle`); on a mic drop the step is 4 px and the burst goes off                                                                                                                                  |
| Flinch   | Cell 4                    | Hold 400 ms, 2 px step back, one frame of a one-shade-lighter overlay                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Mic drop | Burst sheet cells 1 to 3  | The burst between the duellists pulses between cells 1 and 2 at 150 ms while the phrase types, with a white flash frame and a 2 px two-frame shake of the band; still during the exchange; pulses three times on a hit, cell 3 (popped) and gone on a counter                                                             |
| Win      | Cell 5                    | Hold, breathing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Lose     | Cell 6                    | Held, with the breathing cut slowed to a sob (`BREATH.sob`, 1.6 s) and a tear: a two-pixel drop from the eye `features.mjs` found, falling two pixels a frame for five frames every ten (`tearDrop`); the Stakeholder slumps without one (`cries`)                                                                        |

If a sheet comes back with only four usable poses, the fallback is flinch for lose and taunt for win; the spec allows it and the data marks which cells exist. The right-hand duellist is the sheet mirrored; props with text are avoided in the prompts for that reason. The Stakeholder never leaves idle or talk during the barrage; the interrupt is the one time they flinch, and the lose pose is the one time they look up.

The ground under the band is not flat black: the room's last 30 rows are mirrored onto it and faded out downward in a 4 by 4 ordered dither (`Reflection`), so the dark round the window carries the scene's own floor colours for a moment, the way the window's fill has its dithered gradient; the window stays opaque on top of it. UI chrome is built in code, not generated, on the stage's pixel grid so it is pixel art like the room (the bars are DOM like the window, `Hud`, boxes with a pixel of ink around them and a checker of dots in the empty ones; only the room band and the panel's ground are still painted on the canvas): the quiet window (ink, a pale frame, a dark fill with a dithered gradient, a few rows lighter under the frame and a few darker at the bottom edge, the corners knocked out), the bars in the fighting-game arrangement of Street Fighter II and The King of Fighters, so each side reads as one block (each duellist's face in its corner, the scale running from one face to the other, and under each end of it the name with the momentum pips after it; the names and pips first sat in the corners with the scale floating in the middle, and the four pieces did not read as one thing), the shared scale as a beam clash after the beam struggles of Dragon Ball Z: Super Butōden (the player's beam in gold from the left, the opponent's in ember from the right, as many whole pixels a step as fit between the faces, 44 against a colleague and 38 against the Stakeholder, a notch of ink per step, 5 px high, its top level with the faces' frames, meeting at a white spark taller than the beam that slides a step per exchange won, and each duellist's face in the corner at its own end (the head square at two thirds, 16 px, framed in the side's colour, the opponent's mirrored to look at the player), so the beam says whose end is whose; posts capping the ends and two crackling pixels on the spark were tried and looked better gone; winning pushes the spark into the other side's end, so the colour split is the standing; against the Stakeholder the spark starts a step toward the player, who has further to push; a row of ticks with a marker and a pointer came first and did not say who led without reading it), the momentum pips after the names, level with their top (8 by 5 px, filling from the name outward, blinking when full; an empty one has a rim a pixel wide inside its ink in the dots' colour, so the slot reads (a brighter rim was too much)), no bar for the opponent's fatigue on purpose (the colleague just starts fumbling, and noticing it is part of the game; a strip under the pips and a hint explaining it were there and were taken out), the silence bar as a 2 px line under the menu, and a pixel triangle for the cursor. The player's side is gold, the opponent's ember. Text is not drawn into the canvas: the names, the dialogue line, the menus, the hand, the barks and the `Mic drop!` label are DOM in the site's Fira Code, smooth and without a shadow, laid over the stage. The overlay text is 16 px at 3x and never goes below 14 px, so on a small stage the text grows upward past the window; the 70-character limit in Lines fits one row at 3x.

Rendering (layout A of the prototype): the stage is one 320 by 214 pixel grid. The room band on top, rows 0 to 133, shows a 320 by 134 window of the 480 by 270 scene render (`viewFor`: centred on the two stands, the scene's floor line on the stage's feet line at row 128), so the duellists stand at the sheets' own pixels, the room at the render's, everything on one grid; the scene loop sits in the band as an image, offset by the window. The text panel is rows 134 to 213, the quiet window at 3, 136, 314 by 76, so nothing ever covers the people. Every screen sits in the same `Stage`: 3x wherever the viewport is wide enough (the site's 24 px gutter kept), and below that the stage shrinks with the width as a fraction, snapped down to the screen's device pixels where that keeps it above the minimum (`crispScale`: halves on a 2x screen, thirds on a 3x one; a 1x screen keeps the fraction, since snapping there would only shrink the game). On whole device pixels the canvas is drawn at exactly that size (`backingFor`, with `imageSmoothingEnabled = false`) and the scene loop is the 1x frames blown up with hard edges, so nothing is resampled; otherwise the canvas is drawn at 3x and the 8x scene export shown, both scaled down smoothly, which keeps the art's pixels even where hard edges would round them. A short window crops up to 60 rows of ceiling off the duel's band before the stage shrinks for height (`fitFor`), and the duel screen scrolls the stage's bottom into view when it starts.

Held upright (`(orientation: portrait)`, never a width), the stage goes tall (`tallFor`): the full 320 columns across the window's width, and as many rows as the small viewport holds (`100svh`, so Safari's collapsing bars do not move it), at least 214. The room band stays on top at its rows; what is under it grows: the duel's window fills from under the band to a bar of controls along the bottom, the title's room becomes a header that fades into the dark with the menu under it, the role grid's faces grow to three times their pixels with the name and line under them (no standing figure), centred in the rows the title and the bar leave, and the result card stacks. The text is 17 px at least there, the duellists' names a little smaller and the menu rows 44 px, sized for a reader and a thumb rather than from the art's scale (`data-layout="tall"`, the `tall:` variant, `--pb-bar` for what keeps clear of the bar). The bar holds the touch versions of Escape and M: Back or Pause on the left, the sound on the right. On a wide stage under touch the same controls are small pixel buttons, Back in the top left corner, the speaker in the top right, Pause under the middle of the duel's beam; the pause menu has the sound too. A phone on its side (`(orientation: landscape) and (pointer: coarse) and (max-height: 500px)`: its bars leave 290 to 380 px, which a page cannot hide on an iPhone) asks to be turned upright. Where the browser can go fullscreen (Android, desktops; an iPhone cannot), the bar has a fullscreen switch beside the sound, and the turn prompt offers to play sideways fullscreen: the element around every screen goes fullscreen, so it holds from one screen to the next, and the wide stage then fits the screen whole, without the gutter or the duel's crop. Under touch a menu draws no cursor, since there is none to move and a marked row reads as chosen, except on a row whose description is showing. Taps that change what is under the finger act on the click, not the press, so the rest of the tap does not land on the menu that appears (the splash, How to play, leaving the demo). The DOM overlay is positioned in the same frame through a `--px` unit and sizes its text in em. The lab page keeps only the toolbar and the footer around the stage; it does not use the article column.
